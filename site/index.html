Building regulations, cited.

Para answers questions about the building regulations from the documents themselves, and links every statement to the paragraph it came from, so you can check it in one click. It's free, open source and runs on your own computer.

01

Cites the paragraph

Every statement links to its source: ADB Vol 1 Table 3.1, BR 2010 reg 7. Para checks each citation against what the AI actually read and removes any it made up, and tells you it did. It also marks a citation made without reading the whole passage, and any figure that isn't in the passage cited for it.

02

Private by default

With a local engine, your questions and documents never leave your computer. Para shows whether it's private, and says so plainly when you choose an online service instead.

03

Diagrams and tables

About 600 diagrams and tables are shown as images beside their paragraph, and every passage opens the original PDF at its page.

04

Your own documents

Add specifications, fire strategies or building control comments and they're searched with the library. Para asks you to confirm you're allowed to use each one this way first.

How it works

Ask. It looks it up. You check it.

  1. 01
    You ask in plain words: “Minimum headroom over a private stair?” Add notes about your project and every answer takes them into account.
  2. 02
    The AI searches the library and reads the relevant paragraphs, tables and diagrams. You can open the list of everything it looked up, and its reasoning.
  3. 03
    It answers with citations to the paragraphs it read. Click one to open the document at that paragraph, highlighted, with a link to the original.

The library

The documents, not the internet

Approved Documents

A to T and 7, both volumes of B, F, L, M and R, current amendments and corrections, the gov.uk FAQs and the Manual to the Building Regulations.

Legislation

Building Regulations 2010, Building Act 1984, Building Safety Act 2022, the higher-risk building regulations of 2023, the Fire Safety Order 2005 and the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022.

Guidance

Government guidance on higher-risk buildings: the criteria, building control approval, changes, completion, the golden thread and mandatory occurrence reporting.

Rebuilt every week from gov.uk and legislation.gov.uk; the app offers new editions as they're published. England only for now: Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland are planned. Crown copyright, used under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

AI engines

Use your engine, let Para run one, or bring a key

An engine you already run

Lemonade, Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, Jan, vLLM or any other OpenAI-compatible server, on your computer or your office network. Find engines on this computer sets it up for you.

The built-in engine

Para suggests a model that fits your computer's memory, downloads it with llama.cpp, and runs it. Nothing else to install. It works from 8 GB of memory; with 16 GB or more the models get more right. Each model shows how it did on our 36 test questions.

An online AI service

For computers that can't run a model well: Anthropic, Mistral, OpenAI, OpenRouter or another service, with your own API key. Not private: your questions, and passages from your own files, go to that company.

Installing, and the first-run warnings

Mac: unzip, move Para to Applications and open it. The first time, macOS says it can't verify the app: open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

Windows: double-click Para-windows-setup.exe. Windows shows a blue box, Windows protected your PC, with only a Don't run button: click More info under the message, and a Run anyway button appears. Click it. You see this once, when you install; updates don't ask again. Para installs for you alone, with no administrator needed, and adds itself to the Start menu.

Linux: on Ubuntu, Debian or Mint, double-click Para-linux.deb and choose Install. On other distributions, download the AppImage, allow it to run as a program (right-click, Properties) and double-click it. Needs Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 or newer (x86-64). If it can't open its own window, Para opens in your web browser instead.

Windows and macOS show these warnings for apps they don't yet recognise, usually ones from small publishers; they don't mean anything has been found wrong with Para. Para's source is public, and the builds are made from it by GitHub Actions.

Before you rely on it

AI makes mistakes. Para shows its sources so you can check every answer against the documents, but it isn't professional advice and doesn't replace your judgement or building control approval.
